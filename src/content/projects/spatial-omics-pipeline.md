## Problem and motivation

Multiplex tissue imaging produces rich measurements whose interpretation depends on a sequence of processing and quality-control decisions. Reproducibility requires more than running image-analysis tools: intermediate outputs need to remain traceable and reviewable.

## My contribution

I work on computational workflow components for image processing, segmentation-related stages, per-cell feature extraction, phenotype review, and reporting. The aim is to make analysis stages explicit and their outputs inspectable. This description deliberately focuses on general workflow engineering rather than proprietary data or unpublished biological findings.

## Approach and decisions

The workflow uses reproducible orchestration and controlled execution environments. It separates processing stages, records outputs, and supports quality-control review before interpretation. The engineering choices are intended to make failures visible and permit stages to be inspected and repeated.

## Evidence and outputs

Implemented workflow checkpoints include completed processing runs and generated analysis/report outputs. Those checkpoints support claims about workflow execution. They do not independently establish segmentation accuracy, phenotype validity, or a biological conclusion.

## Limitations and current stage

The work is ongoing. Scientific validation must assess image quality, segmentation and annotation, and the downstream biological question separately. This page is an editorial preview pending review of the employer-facing public wording; it is excluded from the production build.
